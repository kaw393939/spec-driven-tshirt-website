# Same brand. Two visual directions.

**Your task:** make two compositions for the same white shirt and audience. Keep the product facts and core message the same so you can compare the design choices.

**Why:** choosing a style label is easy. Showing how a composition communicates your brand is the design work.

## BR-04 · Compare two directions

**Direction A — modernist-inspired:** explore a clear grid, hierarchy, restraint, and function. Show how spacing and type guide attention.

**Direction B — postmodernist-inspired:** explore juxtaposition, expressive type, references, layering, or a deliberately disrupted grid. Explain the meaning behind the disruption.

These are classroom starting points, not full definitions. “Modernist” does not just mean current or trendy. A postmodernist direction still needs readable text and usable controls. Either direction can support any archetype; a justified combination is also possible.

[Try the brand/composition lab](brand-lab.md). Read the [V&A Modernism collection](https://www.vam.ac.uk/collections/modernism) and [V&A’s introduction to Postmodernism](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-postmodernism) alongside your class examples.

## Annotate five choices

For **type, color, imagery, composition, and copy**, write one short reason connecting the choice to your audience and archetype. Include a headline, product image treatment, price, size controls, and a buying action in each sketch.

Example: “The large, quiet headline and open space support our calm voice. The product and price stay easy to scan.” That explains intent; a classmate will help you check whether it comes across.

## BR-05 · Critique, choose, revise

Show both directions before explaining them. Ask a peer:

1. Who does this feel like it is for?
2. What three words describe its personality?
3. Where did you look first, and what was hard to find?

Compare their response with your intent. Choose a direction, record one concrete revision, and save before/after sketches. Disagreement is useful evidence; don’t invent a perfect review.
