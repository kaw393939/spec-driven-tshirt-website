# Why should someone care?

**Your task:** apply two of Cialdini’s principles in places that fit your audience and brand.

**Why:** persuasion is part of the page’s message and structure. Name the intended effect and the evidence that keeps it honest.

## BR-03 · Choose two, then show them

| Principle | A possible expression | The boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | Offer a useful fit or care guide. | No forced signup or purchase. |
| Commitment / consistency | Let people review and edit their choices. | No hidden extras or trapped choices. |
| Social proof | Use genuine, permissioned customer evidence. | Omit it when you have none. |
| Authority | Present accurate, sourced product information. | No invented expert, certification, or claim. |
| Liking | Use a voice and imagery the audience relates to. | No fabricated founder story or endorsement. |
| Scarcity | Explain simulated size availability clearly. | No fake countdowns or scarcity pressure. |
| Unity | Express a welcoming shared interest. | No shaming people into belonging. |

This table is a set of project possibilities. [Read Cialdini’s own explanation](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/); it is not evidence that these ideas will improve this project’s sales.

## Annotate your page

For each choice, write:

```text
Principle:
Where it appears (copy, image, section, or interaction):
What we hope the shopper understands or feels:
Why it fits our audience/archetype:
What supports the claim, or how we avoid making one:
How a peer can check the intended message:
```

> **AI aside · Convincing doesn’t mean true.** I can generate a believable review or “eco-friendly” line. Our plain shirt has no special claim unless you can support it. Keep fictional demo policies labeled.
